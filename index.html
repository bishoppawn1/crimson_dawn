WASD pans the camera. Use the mouse wheel to zoom around the cursor.

Left-click or drag a selection box. Hold Shift to add units.

Right-click terrain to move, an enemy to attack, or an unfinished friendly building to resume construction.

G, then right-click to force-move and ignore enemies until arrival.

Combat units pursue enemies that damage them unless a force-move or explicit attack order takes priority.

Q activates Overdrive on selected assault mechs.

C cancels a selected unfinished building and refunds part of its unbuilt metal.

Buildings block ground movement. Workers automatically slide around them; aircraft fly over terrain and structures.

Units are compact but physical: dense groups spread out instead of stacking in one spot.

Workers construct buildings. Hold Shift while placing to queue multiple foundations in order. Select a factory to queue units; right-click terrain to set their attack-move rally point.

Every factory has four unit roles. Vehicle Factories add Grid Tankers to their scouts, tanks, and artillery; Tier 2–3 Air Factories add Energy Tenders to their interceptors, gunships, and bombers.

Worker construction is grouped by tier. Higher-tier drones inherit lower-tier options; locked buttons show the required worker tier.

A completed Tier 2 or Tier 3 Mech Factory unlocks in-place upgrades to that tier for your existing structures.

Units consume supply when queued. Strategic Supply Complexes add massive capacity and can be upgraded twice.

Ordinary buildings snap to the grid and cannot overlap units or structures. Metal mines snap to marked deposits.

Rocky ridges, crags, and broken starting walls are impassable to ground units and construction. Larger matches use dedicated ring battlefields with nearby expansions and contested central deposits.

Buildings can sit directly beside each other when their visible grid footprints only share an edge.

Tier 1 infrastructure is compact: generators and batteries are 1×1, while Tier 1 factories are 2×2. Higher tiers are larger.

Build mode marks power as filled construction-grid cells. Placement previews say whether the snapped site is inside or outside the grid.

Relay towers extend power. Red rings and crosses mark disconnected structures.

Induction Chargers cover a large area, recharge every unit in their field at once, and fairly share limited grid power.

The energy HUD shows net building power plus stored capacity. Mines draw passive power, and factories draw more while producing.

Generators provide constant power. Batteries store the most surplus; generators and relay towers also hold smaller reserves.

Active units below 18 energy regenerate a slow emergency reserve so they can move or fire again.

All mobile units carry greatly expanded internal energy reserves.

Disabled units slowly build an emergency charge, then reactivate.

Salvage drones can share a wreck and visit nearby piles until their cargo is full.

Aircraft and hovering reclamation drones pass over starting walls; rocky terrain still redirects reclamation drones.

Multiplayer uses a direct host-authoritative peer connection. The host commands the western base; the guest commands the eastern base.
