    FIELD MANUAL

    WASD pans the camera. Use the mouse wheel to zoom around the cursor. Left-click the top-right tactical map to center the camera, or right-click it with units selected to move them across the battlefield.

    Left-click or drag a selection box. Matching factories of the same type and tier can share a selection. Hold Shift to add compatible selections.

    Right-click terrain to move, an enemy to attack, an unfinished friendly building to resume construction, or a damaged friendly target with workers to repair it.

    G, then right-click to force-move and ignore enemies until arrival.

    Combat units pursue enemies that damage them unless a force-move or explicit attack order takes priority.

    Q activates Overdrive on selected assault mechs.

    C cancels a selected unfinished building and refunds part of its unbuilt metal.

    Unit Tester gives the human commander Tier 3 workers, unlimited resources and energy, instant completed buildings, and instant factory production. Its Enemy Spawner can place buildings and units for any AI commander; those assets then follow normal AI, power, energy, and combat rules.

    Buildings block ground movement. Workers automatically slide around them; aircraft fly over terrain, structures, and ground units without pushing them aside.

    Units are compact but physical: dense groups spread out instead of stacking in one spot.

    Workers construct buildings and repair damaged friendly units or completed structures when you right-click the target. A worker can be repaired by another worker, but cannot repair itself. Hold Shift while placing to queue multiple foundations in order. Select a factory to queue units; box-select matching factories to share production controls and automatically route each new unit to the shortest powered queue, then right-click terrain to set their shared attack-move rally point. Selected workers and factories show the active job, live progress, and every next item in queue order.

    Mech and Vehicle Factories each field five tier-matched roles, including Skyguard Mechs and Flak Crawlers for anti-air defense. Air Factories field four faster, lighter aircraft roles.

    Ordinary weapons deal reduced damage to aircraft. Skyguards, Flak Crawlers, and Flak Turrets prioritize aircraft and deal double damage to them.

    The Zenith Doughnut only auto-detects nearby ground targets, then flies directly over and hovers above them while its underbelly beam fires. Distant targets require an explicit order.

    Worker construction is grouped by tier. Higher-tier drones inherit lower-tier options; locked buttons show the required worker tier.

    A completed Tier 2 or Tier 3 Mech Factory unlocks in-place upgrades to that tier for your existing structures.

    Units consume supply when queued. Strategic Supply Complexes add massive capacity and can be upgraded twice.

    Ordinary buildings snap to the grid and cannot overlap units or structures. Metal mines snap to marked deposits.

    Ridges, faults, ancient ruins, and broken starting walls are impassable to ground units and construction. Every player count has several battlefield layouts with landmarks spread from the center into outer expansion routes, plus nearby expansions and contested rich deposits.

    Buildings can sit directly beside each other when their visible grid footprints only share an edge.

    Tier 1 infrastructure is compact: generators and batteries are 1×1, while Tier 1 factories are 2×2. Higher tiers are larger.

    Build mode marks power as filled construction-grid cells. Placement previews say whether the snapped site is inside or outside the grid.

    Relay towers extend power. Red rings and crosses mark disconnected structures.

    Induction Chargers cover a large area, recharge every unit in their field at once, and fairly share limited grid power.

    The energy HUD shows net building power plus stored capacity. Mines draw passive power, and factories draw more while producing.

    Generators provide constant power. Batteries store the most surplus; generators and relay towers also hold smaller reserves.

    Active units below 18 energy regenerate a slow emergency reserve so they can move or fire again.

    All mobile units carry greatly expanded internal energy reserves.

    Disabled units slowly build an emergency charge, then reactivate.

    Salvage drones can share a wreck and visit nearby piles until their cargo is full.

    Aircraft and hovering reclamation drones pass over starting walls; rocky terrain still redirects reclamation drones.

    Multiplayer uses a host-authoritative peer connection. The shared lobby lists the host, guest, and AI bots; the host starts the match and a compatible map is selected randomly.
